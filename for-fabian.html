The No-Cook Kitchen · house edition

A note for Fabian

Flower — this is the short version. Five minutes now, and the kitchen mostly runs itself.

The No-Cook Kitchen is our cookbook, meal planner and shopping list in one page. It knows the rice cooker and the Halo fryer, it has 549 dinners that need almost no cooking, and — the part that matters for you — anything either of us does in it shows up on the other's phone within a few minutes. A recipe you save, a dinner you swap, a tick in the supermarket: shared. There is nothing you can break.

Once only · getting your phone in

You need three things from me. Write them in here if you like:

The cookbook link: The bridge address (ends in /exec): The token (a long password — keep it to ourselves):
  1. Open the cookbook link on your phone. In Safari or Chrome press Share → Add to Home Screen. It becomes an app that works even without signal.
  2. Open it, and tap the little green dot at the top (or any Update shared list button).
  3. Paste the bridge address and the token. Where it says This device is called, write Fabian's phone — that's how the app tells us apart.
  4. Press Save settings, then Test & detect Sheet. It should say Connected.
  5. Press Pull shared data now. Everything I've already saved arrives.

Do the same on a laptop if you want, with a different device name. That's the setup done — forever.

Finding dinner

Scroll, or use the two rows of buttons at the top: the first row picks the machine (rice cooker, Halo, oven, microwave), the second picks the cuisine (Japanese, Korean, Latin American…). They combine. The search box searches everything.

Can't decide? Surprise me picks one for you. Every underlined ingredient opens a card telling you what it is, which brand to buy and where it's cheap.

The week, on autopilot

Plan my week → Standard week deals seven dinners on our rota: Monday European · Tuesday Korean · Wednesday Greek, British, Spanish or Italian · Thursday Japanese · Saturday Latin American · Sunday something new. Takeaway night is a dropdown — Friday unless one of us moves it.

Don't fancy a day? Press its Swap. Want to pick everything yourself? Tick the little box on any card, then use the Manual week. Either way, we both see the same plan.

When you find a recipe you like

This is the most useful thing you can do. On any dish's card:

  1. Press Save recipe.
  2. Paste the web address of the recipe you found.
  3. Press Import from page — it reads the ingredients and method off most recipe sites by itself. If it can't, just type the ingredients, one per line, amount first (400 g beef, 2 onions).
  4. Press Save recipe.

From then on the dish's name links straight to that recipe, the shopping list uses its exact quantities, and it's on my phone in a couple of minutes. The recipe library button at the top lists everything we've saved.

Shopping

  1. In the planner press Grocery list. It merges everything the week needs — 2 onions plus 1 onion becomes 3 onions.
  2. Press Update shared list. It says Confirmed when the Google Sheet has it.
  3. In the shop, use whichever is easier: the Google Sheets app (the Shopping tab — tick column A, ticked things go grey) or the cookbook's own list. Either way, ticks are kept.
  4. Need something that isn't on the list? Type it in the Anything else box, or straight into the bottom of the Sheet — those rows are never overwritten.
  5. Home again: press Ticked → pantry. Everything you bought moves into the pantry list in one go.

The pantry

The Pantry box under the shopping list is what we already have — one thing per line, with an amount if you know it (500 ml mentsuyu, rice). The list subtracts it automatically: if a recipe wants 400 ml and we have 500, mentsuyu quietly disappears from the shopping. Delete a line when something runs out. It's shared, so whoever notices we're out of soy sauce fixes it for both of us.

Looking back

Past weeks is our food diary — every week either of us has sent to the Sheet, printed or put in the calendar. Press Load as manual week on any of them to have that week again. Calendar file drops next week's dinners into your phone calendar at 7pm, if you like that sort of thing.

The only three rules

  • You can't break it. Every change merges; the newer one wins; nothing is ever lost. The green dot's tooltip and the Recent sync activity list show exactly what came from where.
  • If something looks missing, tap the green dot and press Pull shared data now. That fixes almost everything.
  • The token is ours. Share the cookbook link with anyone you like; keep the token and the bridge address between us. The Sheet is only editable with both.
